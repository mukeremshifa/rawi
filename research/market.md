# Rawi market and initial segment research

## Recommendation

Start with a narrow learning outcome for **18+ students taking one shared introductory university course in the UAE**: “Find the concepts you cannot yet apply, practice them using your course materials, and check that you can handle a fresh question independently.” Introductory microeconomics is a plausible default because it combines conceptual explanations, graphs and bounded application questions. Choose the actual course by overlap among recruited learners, available materials and access to a capable reviewer. This is a provisional product judgment, not validated demand or an established competitive advantage.

The founder can reach UAE college students aged 17–24 studying social sciences, business, economics, biology, chemistry, mathematics and physics, plus high-school students aged 13–19. Those relationships favor a course-specific study product over an introductory programming tutor. Plan the initial pilot around the 18+ subset; 17-year-old college students and younger pupils are a later, explicit expansion with an appropriate product and safeguarding review. College enrollment must not be treated as proof of adulthood.

English is the first delivery language; Arabic should follow demonstrated need and adequate evaluation. The working constraints are a solo founder with at least 20 hours weekly, zero hosting/deployment charges, willingness to pay for AI, and a real small pilot. Prioritize one complete learning loop under those constraints. UAE access is an operational advantage to test, not evidence that a broad local market is underserved.

The evidence supports entering a real category but does not support building a general AI tutor for everyone. HEPI’s March 2026 report, based on 1,054 full-time UK undergraduates surveyed by Savanta in December 2025, found 95% reported some AI use. That establishes adoption in this population, not worldwide market size, unmet demand, or willingness to pay for Rawi. The report was sponsored by education technology provider Kortext. [1]

## Competitive baseline

Product descriptions reflect official sources available on 13 September 2026, establishing advertised capabilities, not learning efficacy. Absence from a page does not prove a feature is missing.

| Product | Verified capabilities and access | Implication for Rawi |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT Study Mode | OpenAI’s current help describes global availability across plans on web, iOS and Android; guided questions, explanations, understanding checks, uploaded course materials and optional memory. Normal plan limits apply, and it can still make mistakes or give direct answers. [2] | Socratic dialogue, personalization and material uploads are baseline capabilities. A student needs a reason to leave a familiar general assistant. |
| NotebookLM / Gemini Notebook | Google’s live support now uses “Gemini Notebook”; older announcements use NotebookLM. Current documentation covers uploaded sources, grounded chat with citations and transformed study materials. Its 2025 Learning Guide announcement includes open questions, adaptive explanations, quizzes and flashcards. Standard access is free. [3–5] | “Upload PDFs and study with an AI” is directly occupied. Citations alone are not a differentiator. |
| Khanmigo | Integrates tutoring with Khan Academy content and offers parent chat review and alerts. The live parent page lists US$4/month or US$44/year before tax, with a U.S. residence and billing-address requirement for direct parent/learner subscriptions. Under-18 access requires a parent or guardian. [6] | A low consumer price and an established curriculum make broad school tutoring difficult to enter without a specific advantage. |
| Quizlet | Free study tools plus paid Plus and Plus Unlimited tiers. Official support describes study guides, practice tests, Learn, Expert Solutions, custom study paths, progress and smart grading; Plus has usage limits. [7] | Flashcards, AI-generated revision and progress views are already a mature bundle. Numeric pricing is omitted because direct checkout verification was unavailable. |
| StudyFetch | The current homepage advertises course-material uploads, tutoring, study schedules, lecture notes, games, visuals and practice tests; its tutor is now called Sparky. A free entry point is advertised, but current paid pricing was not verified. [8] | An attractive course study workspace and study schedule are insufficient positioning by themselves. |

Several apparently promising differentiators are already moving targets. Khan Academy announced interactive math/science diagrams and teacher-curated practice with reporting in August 2026. Google has published education integrations with Classroom, Canvas and Schoology. StudyFetch maintains an official campus ambassador page describing campus campaigns and social content. These show competitors building both richer learning workflows and routes to learners; they do not prove those channels’ economics or effectiveness. [9–11]

Current Google help also contains a transition worth handling carefully: a legacy daily-limit table remains on the upgrade page, while its specific usage page announces compute-based limits from 2 September 2026, with five-hour refreshes and a weekly limit. This memo therefore makes no numeric quota comparison. Product names, access and entitlements should be checked again immediately before customer-facing comparisons. [5,12]

## Three candidate starting segments

The ratings are analytical judgments about a solo-builder release with the founder’s recruitment access, not measured market scores. All three face substantial competition.

| Criterion | One conceptual university course | One numerical math/physics unit | Introductory programming |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial fit | Strongest overlap with the stated audience; actual shared enrollment still needs confirmation. | Plausible among the reachable math/physics learners. | No recruitment advantage identified; not recommended initially. |
| Concrete problem | “I know the definitions but cannot apply them to a new situation.” | “I can follow a solution but cannot choose and execute the method.” | “I understand an explanation but cannot write the next program.” |
| Independent assessment | Reviewed scenarios and short explanations; avoid unrestricted essay grading initially. | Constrained numeric/symbolic items; valid alternative working is harder to grade. | Strong for bounded programs, though passing tests does not prove understanding. |
| Content burden | Manageable for one module; syllabus coverage and question validity need human review. | Higher burden for notation, units, worked steps, diagrams and equivalent forms. | Manageable concept bank, but creates avoidable audience-discovery work. |
| Competitive pressure | Direct overlap with Gemini Notebook, Quizlet and StudyFetch. | Khanmigo and general study assistants. | General coding assistants and course-owned tutors. |
| Provisional choice | Default if a common course and useful materials are confirmed. | Alternative if common enrollment and review expertise are stronger. | Defer unless actual learner demand changes the evidence. |

**A conceptual course grounded in its materials.** Begin with one module from an introductory course shared by several recruits. Microeconomics is a candidate, not an instruction to build an entire economics curriculum. Supply and demand, opportunity cost or elasticity could support short explanations followed by fresh scenarios. Even these concepts require careful graph and calculation checks; source grounding does not make generated assessments correct. Introductory biology, business or another subject may win the discovery gate instead.

The promise should be specific: a learner discovers a misconception, practices it, then applies the concept without assistance. Compare that experience with Gemini Notebook and StudyFetch using the same permitted materials. Citations and uploaded slides remain necessary infrastructure, not the claim of uniqueness. Avoid high-stakes clinical content, arbitrary whole-textbook ingestion and unrestricted long-answer grading in the pilot.

**A numerical math or physics unit.** This can be a stronger starting point when several reachable learners share the same unit and a reviewer can verify methods, units and alternate solutions. Numeric answers make some checks easier, while step assessment, handwriting and diagram interpretation expand the workload. Pick a narrow set of typed-input problems first if this segment wins. A model’s plausible explanation is not sufficient evidence of mathematical correctness.

**Programming as a deferred option.** Software expertise alone is a weak reason to choose an audience the founder has not identified. Course-specific tool policies also matter across all subjects: CS50x 2026, for example, permits its own AI software while restricting other AI software that suggests or completes answers or code. The UAE pilot needs its own institution and course policy checks; this U.S. example is not evidence of UAE rules. [13]

## What could become defensible

No prompt, model choice or feature bundle constitutes a moat here. The plausible advantage is cumulative execution in a narrow context: a reviewed skill graph; a bank of misconception-sensitive practice tasks; feedback from real attempts; a reliable progression policy; and access to a learner community. These are hypotheses about how Rawi could become valuable, not claims that competitors lack them.

The central product object should be a **skill with evidence**. For each skill, distinguish “not assessed,” “completed with help,” “demonstrated independently” and “needs review.” Show why a practice activity is recommended. Do not present a precise mastery percentage merely because the model can generate one. An answer copied from an explanation should not count the same as a fresh unaided solution.

The first complete experience could take 15–20 minutes: select a course concept, attempt a short diagnostic, work through one activity with optional hints and source references, solve a fresh scenario without assistance, and receive a small next-session plan. Those durations are design hypotheses. Judge success by independent performance and return behavior rather than chat volume.

## Demand, distribution and pricing tests

Run discovery before a large build. Recruit 8–12 adults from the reachable UAE students and map their actual course enrollment, upcoming assessment topics, permitted materials and existing tools. Ask for a recent episode: what they attempted, where they became stuck and what remained unresolved. Observe five learners use their present solution. These are proposed discovery counts, not a representative UAE market study.

The first gate is course overlap: find roughly five or more eligible learners studying the same course or sufficiently aligned module, with usable materials and a reviewer for the questions. This is a practical recruitment threshold, not a demand statistic. If microeconomics lacks overlap, choose the strongest shared course; do not force the provisional subject. If no course clusters, broaden interviews before implementing subject-specific content.

After readiness checks, run a free two-week pilot with 10–20 adults, including at least eight studying the same unit. The five-person overlap gate selects a prototype course; the eight-person shared-unit gate supports a more coherent full pilot. Give each learner a narrow objective, an initial assessment, several practice sessions and fresh assessment items. Compare a portion of the experience with their existing tool using matched topics where practical. Counterbalance order or acknowledge order effects. Satisfaction without evidence of learning or a voluntary return is weak justification for continued development.

Use the founder’s existing relationships first, then introductions through a course club, peer tutor or student society where recruitment is permitted. Do not assume institutional procurement is a quick route. Record the university and course context rather than treating UAE colleges as a single homogeneous audience. If focused recruitment cannot produce a small cohort, revise the course before adding features.

Test willingness to pay after the free pilot demonstrates repeat value, as a separate monetization experiment. Present a concrete offer in the cohort’s actual currency and region; seek a purchase or clearly disclosed refundable reservation only when charging is authorized and operationally ready. Do not infer willingness to pay from “I would use this.” Free incumbent access and Khanmigo’s low price complicate a generic premium tutor claim; they do not determine Rawi’s price. Avoid unlimited paid usage until cost per completed learning session is understood.

Suggested continuation gates are deliberately modest and provisional: most pilot learners finish one full loop, a meaningful subset returns voluntarily within seven days, and several demonstrate improvement on fresh unaided items. Record exact counts and failures. Payment is not a first-pilot gate. Small samples are for choosing the next experiment, not advertising proven grade improvement.

## Limits and next decision

This research does not establish UAE market size, conversion, retention, cost of acquisition or causal benefit. UAE AI investment and enthusiasm would not establish unmet student demand. Official pages favor their own products; testimonials are not controlled efficacy studies. HEPI findings apply to its UK sample. StudyFetch paid pricing and Quizlet checkout prices were not directly verified. The pilot’s legal and age-verification requirements need separate review.

The provisional default is one English-language introductory course for an 18+ UAE cohort, with microeconomics as a candidate. The first decision is whether actual course overlap, review expertise and materials support it. Broader teen access, Arabic, additional subjects, school sales and voice tutoring should follow evidence that the first cohort benefits and returns.
