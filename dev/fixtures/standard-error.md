# Worked problems: the standard error

These problems are fictional. The numbers are chosen so each one can be checked by hand.

## The formula

The **standard error of a mean** is the standard deviation of the sample mean across repeated samples. It is estimated from one sample as

$$
SE = \frac{s}{\sqrt{n}}
$$

where s is the sample standard deviation and n is the number of respondents.

## Computing an interval

To build an approximate 95 per cent interval for a mean:

1. Compute the sample mean and the sample standard deviation.
2. Divide the standard deviation by the square root of the sample size to get the standard error.
3. Multiply the standard error by 1.96.
4. Add and subtract that margin from the sample mean.

## A worked example

A survey of 225 tenants reports a mean monthly rent of 840 pounds with a sample standard deviation of 150 pounds. The standard error is 150 divided by 15, which is 10 pounds. The margin is 1.96 times 10, or 19.6 pounds, so the interval runs from 820.4 to 859.6 pounds.

## Why quadrupling the sample halves the error

Because n sits under a square root, a sample four times as large has a standard error half as large. A survey of 900 tenants with the same spread would have a standard error of 5 pounds.
