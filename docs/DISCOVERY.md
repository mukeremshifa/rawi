# Rawi discovery and adult pilot

Status: proposed research protocol, 13 September 2026. No recruitment messages have been sent. The questions and gates below are product decisions, not established educational benchmarks.

Rawi's intended audience includes UAE college students aged 17–24 across social sciences, business, economics, and introductory maths/science, with later high-school support for ages 13–19. Recruit 10–20 consenting adults aged 18+ for this first pilot. English is primary; record language preferences and difficulties rather than assuming every learner has the same proficiency. A college student aged 17 remains outside this adult pilot.

## Ten interview questions

Ask about recent behavior before showing the product. Request examples the participant is permitted to share; do not request account passwords or private class-group conversations.

1. Which course and specific topic caused you the most difficulty in the last two weeks, and what happened when you tried to study it?
2. Walk me through the last time you got stuck, from your first attempt until you stopped or found help.
3. Which notes, websites, videos, free AI tools, friends, or tutors did you actually use, and what did each contribute?
4. Show an example of an explanation that seemed clear but did not help you solve the next question. What was still confusing?
5. After getting help, how did you check whether you could solve a new question independently?
6. What does your next assessment require: definitions, calculations, interpreting graphs, short explanations, essays, or something else?
7. Which parts of your course materials may you share for a private pilot, and who could confirm permission where ownership or reuse is unclear?
8. When and where do you study, which device do you use, and what language, accessibility, or connectivity issues interrupt you?
9. What have you paid for learning help recently, if anything, and what made that expense worthwhile or disappointing?
10. After trying both experiences, which would you choose for your next real study session, why, and what would prevent you from returning?

## Choose the first course

Create an anonymized matrix of course, topic, upcoming assessment, recent difficulty, current alternative, and willingness to attend a follow-up. Prefer the largest reachable group sharing the same course unit and a near-term reason to study. Introductory microeconomics is provisional: opportunity cost, demand/supply shifts, and elasticity offer conceptual and numeric checks. Change this choice if another course has stronger overlap. Secure a subject-competent reviewer before writing a larger curriculum.

Begin with 8–12 discovery conversations. Five adults sharing a course/aligned unit, a reviewer, and source permissions justify prototype selection. Target at least eight learners sharing the unit within the full 10–20-person pilot. Five prototype participants are not a claim that the full cohort is recruited.

Keep interview participation distinct from product enrollment. Explain what is recorded, who reviews it, how long it is retained, and how participants can withdraw or request deletion. Get separate consent for screen/audio recording; note-taking should remain available. Incentives, if used, should compensate participation rather than positive feedback or high scores.

For course notes, record author/owner, permission or license, allowed use, expiry if any, and permission to process through an external AI provider. A learner's access to a textbook or slides does not establish permission to redistribute or ingest them. Use original reviewed examples when rights are uncertain. Exclude restricted assessment questions, answer keys, names, grades, and other students' information. Public readability alone does not qualify a source for ingestion.

## Pilot protocol

**Before the first session:** freeze and timestamp the protocol, metric definitions, item bank, rubrics, and versions. Prepare two equivalent mini-lessons with separate unseen checks. Have the reviewer verify numeric answers, conceptual assumptions, acceptable explanations, and difficulty. For elasticity, explicitly define percentage-change method, sign convention, and rounding. Use fictional figures for local examples unless a factual source is provided.

**Initial session, approximately 40–50 minutes:** confirm eligibility and consent; conduct the behavioral interview; collect a short unaided baseline; observe one Rawi task and one equivalent task using the participant's usual free tool. Alternate which tool and topic appear first across participants. Give comparable time allowances and source availability. Record exact free tool, version/tier where visible, settings, date, and whether limits affected access. Do not train participants to misuse the comparison tool. This comparison is for usability and preference; topic differences, practice carryover, and small sample size prevent a strong efficacy inference.

After each activity, administer a fresh independent check. Permit “I do not know.” No hints, answer reveals, or other tools during that attempt; if help is requested, switch to practice and mark it assisted. Give feedback after submission. Record whether observation altered the participant's normal behavior and whether the founder had to intervene.

**Follow-up:** invite the agreed voluntary study return and schedule one seven-day check, with a recorded window of day 6–9. Log reminders separately from voluntary returns. Ask about outside practice, AI use, and exam preparation since the session. Offer fresh unseen items before tutoring. Record withdrawals and missing follow-ups without replacing them with assumed zero scores or silently deleting participants.

## Definitions to preregister

| Metric | Definition fixed before data collection |
|---|---|
| Activation | Consented participants who complete one learn/practice/check loop; report numerator over all who start a session |
| Founder-independent completion | Full loops completed without instructional or navigation intervention; report failures and reasons |
| Immediate independent result | Rubric score on first submission of fresh check items with no assistance; report assisted attempts separately |
| Delayed independent result | Score on fresh items in the day 6–9 window; report observed scores and follow-up completion over everyone due |
| Return | A substantive practice/check/review event on a different day within seven days; report prompted and unprompted returns separately |
| Preference | Tool selected for an optional next study task, plus the participant's reason; distinguish stated preference from actual use |
| Cost | All inference and job costs, including failures/retries, divided by completed loops; also report total cost and incomplete loops |
| Tutor defect | Reviewer-confirmed factual error, harmful misconception, unjustified rejection, answer leakage, or unrecoverable feedback; report counts and severity |

Preserve the original registration and log every later change with its rationale. Do not change success thresholds after seeing weak results. Use pseudonymous participant IDs and keep contact information separate from learning records.

## Decision after 10–20 participants

Review each failed loop, incorrect explanation, missed follow-up, and preference for the existing tool. The current provisional product gates are at least 7/10 completing without founder help, half returning for a meaningful event, and at least five voluntarily choosing another Rawi session; [PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md) owns the current definitions and any later revisions. These gates identify whether further work is justified, not statistical significance.

Continue only if the loop works, the material is accurate, and a recurring need emerges. Improve the loop if students like explanations but cannot handle fresh questions. Change course selection if recruitment overlap is weak. Report individual outcomes, denominators, uncertainty, outside-study exposure, and dropouts. Reserve causal learning claims for a later adequately powered, preregistered randomized study with an appropriate comparison.
